Plan Approval and CMDA Permits in Chennai — The 2026 Process Explained

23 June 2026 · 7 min read ·by Suppliable team

You cannot legally start construction on any plot in Chennai without an approved building plan. Doing so risks demolition, double-the-normal fees as penalty, registration cancellation, and difficulty selling or getting a home loan later. This post walks through the CMDA approval process in 2026, what documents you need, what it costs, and where most first-time builders trip up.

Who issues the approval — CMDA, DTCP, or local body?

Chennai jurisdiction is split:

  • CMDA (Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority) — for plots within the Chennai Metropolitan Area (CMA), which covers most of central Chennai, OMR, ECR up to a notified extent, and surrounding villages
  • DTCP (Directorate of Town and Country Planning) — for areas outside the CMA in Tamil Nadu, including parts of Tambaram, Chengalpattu, Kanchipuram suburbs
  • Local body (corporation / municipality / panchayat) — for the final issuance of building permit (separate from layout/plan approval)

The two-step structure: CMDA approves the plan, then the local body issues the building permit based on that approval.

Most plots inside Chennai city + OMR up to Siruseri + ECR up to Akkarai fall under CMDA. When in doubt, search your plot's address on the CMDA portal or ask a local surveyor.

Documents you need

For a single residential building plan approval:

From you (the owner)

  • Patta (land ownership record from TN revenue dept)
  • EC (Encumbrance Certificate) — proves no pending mortgages, for the past 13 years
  • Sale deed (original + registered copy)
  • Property tax receipt (latest)
  • Aadhaar + PAN of all co-owners
  • OSR (Open Space Reservation) compliance if plot is in a layout
  • No-objection certificate (NOC) from neighbours if shared wall is involved
  • Affidavit as per CMDA prescribed format

From your architect / engineer

  • Site plan (showing plot boundaries, surrounding roads, neighbouring buildings)
  • Building plan (floor-wise layout, dimensions, setbacks)
  • Structural drawings (foundation, beam, column, slab details)
  • Elevation and section drawings
  • Soil test report (mandatory for buildings G+1 and above, or in coastal areas)
  • Architect's signature + registration number (must be a Council-of-Architecture-registered architect)
  • Structural engineer's certificate for buildings above G+1

CMDA online plan approval — the 2026 process

CMDA moved entirely online via the OSR (Online Single-Window Registration) portal at https://onlineppa.tn.gov.in. The flow:

Step 1 — Architect submits the plan

Your architect uploads all drawings + your documents to the OSR portal. Plans must be in CAD format with CMDA-standard layers. Initial submission fee: ₹3,000 – 7,500 depending on plot size.

Step 2 — Scrutiny by CMDA

CMDA's planning officer reviews the submission. Typical objections:

  • Setback violations (front, sides, rear) — Chennai requires minimum 1.5 m on sides for plots under 240 sqm; more for bigger plots
  • FSI (Floor Space Index) exceeded — residential FSI in Chennai is typically 1.5 - 2.0 depending on zone and road width
  • Height violations — limited by road width (height ≤ 1.5 × road width is a common rule)
  • Ground coverage exceeded — usually max 50-65% of plot area
  • Missing parking provision — 1 car park mandatory per dwelling unit above 100 sqm

If objections are raised, your architect resubmits with corrections. This back-and-forth typically takes 30-60 days in 2026.

Step 3 — Fee payment

Once CMDA accepts the plan, you pay:

Fee component Approx. range (1000 sqft house)
Scrutiny fee ₹3,000 – 7,500
Development charges (CMDA) ₹15,000 – 30,000
OSR contribution (if applicable) ₹10,000 – 50,000
Premium FSI (if any) varies
Infrastructure charges ₹5,000 – 15,000
Local body fees ₹8,000 – 20,000
Total approval cost ₹40,000 – ₹1,20,000

For bigger plots (above 750 sqm), OSR + infra charges scale up significantly. For commercial / G+2+ residential, expect ₹1.5 – 3+ lakh in approval fees.

Step 4 — Approval letter + plan stamping

CMDA issues the approval letter and stamps the building plan. You take both to your local body (corporation zone office, municipality, or panchayat) to obtain the building permit. Local body adds 2-4 weeks and another fee tranche.

Step 5 — Construction commencement

You can legally start construction. CMDA requires you to upload progress photos at stages (foundation, plinth, lintel, roof) via the OSR portal. Inspectors may visit during construction.

Step 6 — Completion certificate

After construction, the architect certifies completion and uploads as-built drawings. CMDA + local body issue the Completion Certificate (CC). Without this, you cannot legally occupy the building, register it for property tax at the current value, or sell it without complications.

Realistic timeline

Stage Time
Document collection by owner 1-3 weeks
Architect prepares drawings 2-3 weeks
First CMDA scrutiny 2-4 weeks
Objections + resubmission cycle 4-8 weeks
Final approval + fee payment 1-2 weeks
Local body building permit 2-4 weeks
Total before construction starts 3-5 months

Plan accordingly. Most first-time builders underestimate this and start losing money on rent for 4 months while waiting.

Common rejection / delay reasons

  1. Plot is in a CRZ (Coastal Regulation Zone) area — ECR builds south of Akkarai face stricter scrutiny; CRZ-II clearance from Tamil Nadu Coastal Zone Management Authority is needed for plots within 200 m of the high-tide line
  2. Plot doesn't have approved layout — some older Chennai plots are in unapproved layouts; you need to regularise the layout first
  3. Patta doesn't match registered area — survey + patta correction adds 6-12 weeks
  4. Adjoining plot dispute — neighbours objecting causes hold
  5. Architect not Council-of-Architecture registered
  6. OSR contribution not paid by the layout promoter — falls on you to clear retroactively
  7. Plan exceeds permissible FSI — most common, requires plan redesign

Cost-saving tip: don't skip the architect

Many first-time builders try to use a "draughtsman" or "civil engineer" instead of a registered architect to save ₹30-60k in design fees. This routinely backfires:

  • CMDA rejects plans without architect sign-off
  • Structural mistakes cost lakhs to fix post-construction
  • Resale value drops because future buyers ask for architect-stamped plans

Spend ₹40-80k on a proper architect. They more than recover that cost in correctly designed setbacks, structural efficiency, and approval-cycle speed.

Building without approval — the consequences

If you build without CMDA approval and get caught (usually triggered by a neighbour complaint or sale-time legal scrutiny):

  • Penalty fee — typically 2-3× the regular approval fee, plus arrears for the construction period
  • Demolition order for unauthorised portions (entire floors above sanctioned height get demolished)
  • Property cannot be sold or mortgaged until regularised
  • Banks refuse home loans on the property
  • Building cannot be registered for legal electricity / water connection at residential rates (or sometimes at all)
  • Tax department flags the construction value as undeclared expenditure for IT scrutiny

There are periodic "regularisation schemes" (the most recent being announced in 2017-19), but waiting for the next one is a bad bet.

Frequently asked

How long does CMDA approval take in 2026?

Realistically 3-5 months from architect engagement to building permit. Faster (2-3 months) if your plot is clean, architect is experienced, and there are no objections.

Can I get plan approval for a plot I haven't bought yet?

No — you need the registered sale deed in your name (or with a registered power of attorney). However, you can have an architect prepare a preliminary feasibility study before purchase to confirm what you can build.

Is CMDA approval needed for a 600 sqft house?

Yes — any new construction requires approval regardless of size. The fee scales down for smaller plots but the process is the same.

What if my plot is in DTCP, not CMDA?

DTCP follows similar process but slightly different fee structure and online portal (https://www.tnopc.org). Documents required are largely the same.

What's the FSI for residential plots in Chennai?

Generally 1.5 to 2.0, varying by zone and road width. Premium FSI up to 3.25 is available in some commercial / mixed-use zones by paying additional fees. Check the CMDA's land-use plan for your specific zone.

Can I add a floor later without re-approval?

Any vertical addition requires fresh CMDA approval. Trying to do it without is the most common cause of post-construction CMDA demolition notices.
