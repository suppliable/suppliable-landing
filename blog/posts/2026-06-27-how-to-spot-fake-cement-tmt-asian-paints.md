India's counterfeit construction materials market is estimated at **₹50,000+ crore a year** — roughly 8-12% of total industry sales. The biggest victims are residential builders and contractors who buy on price alone, often through informal channels, and only discover they bought fake material when:

- Concrete fails strength tests
- Paint peels in the first monsoon
- TMT bars rust through within 5 years
- Brand warranty claims get rejected because the batch isn't theirs

This post walks through how to identify counterfeit **cement (UltraTech, Ramco), TMT steel (Tata Tiscon, ARS), and Asian Paints + Birla Opus** — the three most-counterfeited categories — with concrete checks anyone on site can do.

## Why counterfeiting works in construction

Three reasons unique to this industry:

1. **Performance is delayed.** A fake paint, fake cement, fake steel — none of these fail immediately. Cement weakness shows up in year 3. Paint failure in year 1-2. Steel corrosion in year 4-6. By the time you discover, the supplier is untraceable.
2. **Brand premium creates margin.** UltraTech cement sells at ₹410. A counterfeit refilled bag costs the fraudster ₹250 to source + repack. ₹160 margin per bag is too tempting.
3. **Trust-based buying.** Most contractors buy by relationship, not verification. Once a shop is "trusted", buyers stop checking.

## Fake cement — UltraTech and Ramco

### How fakes are made

- **Refilling**: Buy real-brand empty bags from waste collectors (₹20-50/bag), fill with low-grade cement or 60-40% cement-fly ash mix, reseal
- **Cloning**: Print fake bags that mimic UltraTech / Ramco packaging — colour, logo, batch code patterns
- **Diversion**: Real brand cement sold without invoice, often with tampered manufacturing dates to clear old stock

### How to spot

**1. Bag construction**
- Real UltraTech / Ramco bags use **woven HDPE with PP laminate** — feels stiff, crinkles audibly
- Fake bags often use cheaper woven plastic — softer, less crinkly, sometimes fading on the printed logo
- Check the **seam stitching**: real factory bags have evenly-spaced machine-sewn seams. Refills have uneven stitches or glue residue
- Check the **printed batch code**: should be **factory-printed in continuous serial**, not pasted-on stickers (a sticker is suspicious)

**2. Manufacturing date check**
- Every legal cement bag must show **manufacturing date in DD/MM/YYYY format**
- Cement loses strength after 90 days from manufacture even when stored properly
- If the bag has no date or a clearly tampered date, refuse it

**3. Batch verification (free)**

- **UltraTech**: SMS the batch number printed on the bag to **56767** (in format: ULBATCH<space><batch number>), or use the **UltraTech BUILD app** → "Verify Batch". You'll get an SMS confirming whether the batch is genuine and which plant it shipped from
- **Ramco**: Open the **Ramco Cement app** → "Verify Product" → scan the QR code on the bag. Confirmation appears in seconds
- **Dalmia**: Use https://www.dalmiacement.com/verify-product with batch number
- **Birla**: Email batch number to customercare@birlasupercement.com

**A real bag's batch will verify. A counterfeit batch returns "not found" or returns a date/plant that doesn't match the bag.**

**4. The setting test (slow, but conclusive)**

- Mix a small handful (50 g) of cement with water at 0.4 water-cement ratio in a plastic cup
- Real PPC starts initial setting in **30-45 minutes**, final set in **6-10 hours**
- Fake cement (filled with fly ash + filler) sets either too fast (< 20 min, often crumbly) or never properly hardens

This is overkill for daily orders but useful when you suspect a whole batch.

**5. Cube test (most rigorous)**

Cast 3 cubes (150 mm × 150 mm × 150 mm) at standard mix, send to a NABL-accredited lab. Strength below BIS minimum = fake or sub-spec batch. Cost ₹500-1000 per cube; results in 7-28 days. Worth doing for any large pour or any time you're uncertain about supply.

## Fake TMT steel — Tata Tiscon, ARS, ISteel

This is harder to spot than fake cement because steel is steel — but the **grade markings, weight per metre, and corrosion resistance** vary significantly between real and fake.

### How fakes are made

- **Re-rolling mills** (small backyard operations) buy scrap steel, re-roll into TMT bars without proper heat treatment or alloy control
- They **emboss "Tata Tiscon", "ARS", "Tata SD" markings** on the rib pattern — looks identical to genuine bars
- Or they buy genuine ungraded steel and embellish it with fake brand stamps
- Sold at ₹3-5/kg below real brand pricing, sometimes through "wholesale" channels

### How to spot

**1. Brand-embossed markings (visual)**

Every genuine TMT bar has the **brand mark stamped at regular intervals (~1 m apart)** along its length, in the rib pattern. For real bars:

- **Tata Tiscon**: stamped "TATA TISCON" + grade (e.g., 500D) + heat number, evenly spaced
- **ARS Steels**: "ARS" + diameter + grade, every 1 m
- **ISteel**: "ISTEEL" or "IS" + grade
- **JSW Neosteel**: "JSW NEO" + grade

Fake bars often have **fading or inconsistent embossing** — the spacing between marks varies, or the marks are shallower than genuine

**2. The bend test (free, immediate)**

Real Fe 500D / Fe 550D should bend through 180° without cracking. Cut a 30 cm length and bend by hand or with a vice. If it cracks at the bend, it's likely:
- Low-carbon steel masquerading as high-grade TMT
- Improperly heat-treated re-rolled steel

Genuine TMT will bend cleanly without surface cracks.

**3. Weight per metre check (D²/162)**

For a given diameter, weight per metre is governed by the formula **D² / 162**:

| Diameter | Real weight/m | Fake-bar typical |
|---|---|---|
| 8 mm | 0.395 kg | 0.35-0.38 kg |
| 10 mm | 0.617 kg | 0.55-0.60 kg |
| 12 mm | 0.889 kg | 0.80-0.85 kg |
| 16 mm | 1.580 kg | 1.42-1.50 kg |

Fakes are often **5-10% under-weight** because they're rolled to slightly smaller diameter to save steel. Measure a 1-m length on a kitchen scale and compare to the expected value. Even simpler: a 12-m rod of genuine 12 mm Fe 500D weighs **10.67 kg**. If your bundle's per-rod weight is 9.5 kg, it's under-sized.

See our [TMT Weight Chart](/blog/tmt-steel-weight-chart-with-worked-examples/) for the full reference.

**4. Brand verification**

- **Tata Tiscon**: SMS the heat number (stamped on the rib) to **5616108** or use the Tata Tiscon Connect app
- **JSW Neosteel**: Use the JSW App's QR scanner
- **ARS**: Their website has a batch verification tool at https://arsteel.in/verify (works for orders shipped from approved depots)

**5. Mill Test Certificate (MTC)**

A genuine brand-channel TMT order comes with a **Mill Test Certificate** — a one-page PDF showing the chemical composition (carbon, sulphur, phosphorus content), mechanical properties (yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, elongation), and the batch/heat number. If your supplier can't produce the MTC for the bars they're delivering, that's a serious red flag.

**6. Corrosion resistance (long-term but visible)**

Real CRS (Corrosion Resistant Steel) shows much slower surface rust than standard Fe 500D. After 4 weeks of outdoor exposure:
- Genuine CRS: light surface oxidation, easily wire-brushed off
- Fake "CRS" (regular steel with paint or label): heavy uniform rust, pitting

## Fake Asian Paints and Birla Opus

Paint counterfeiting is the most lucrative (high MRP, low production cost) and also the easiest to detect once you know what to look for.

### How fakes are made

- **Refilling**: Empty Asian Paints / Birla Opus cans are bought from painters / waste channels, refilled with cheap acrylic distemper, resealed
- **Cloning**: Cheap paint repackaged in lookalike cans with fake brand printing
- **Tinting fraud**: Cheap white paint tinted in a brand's machine but sold as a higher-tier brand product (e.g., Tractor Emulsion sold as Royale)

### How to spot

**1. Can construction**

- Genuine Asian Paints cans are **rolled-edge metal** for buckets, **moulded HDPE for plastic packs**
- Counterfeits often use thinner metal that dents easily, or HDPE with visible seam lines
- Check the **lid seal**: Asian Paints uses a heat-sealed inner foil — peel back the plastic lid and you should see a foil seal. No foil = refill suspect
- Birla Opus uses tamper-evident screw caps with a security ring — if the ring is broken or missing, the can was opened before sale

**2. Batch code and manufacturing date**

- Real Asian Paints cans have **batch code + manufacturing date + MRP printed on the can in machine-printed format** (usually on the base or near the lid)
- The print should be **clear, sharp, not smudged**
- Tampered or sticker-applied dates = suspect

**3. App-based verification**

- **Asian Paints**: Use the **Asian Paints app** → "Genuine Product Check" → enter the batch code printed on the can. Returns confirmation.
- **Birla Opus**: Open the **Birla Opus app** or website https://birlaopus.com/genuine-check → enter the can serial number
- **Dulux**: AkzoNobel app has similar batch verification

**4. The smell + colour test (subtle)**

Genuine acrylic emulsion has a faint, almost mild "watery acrylic" smell. Counterfeit paint often smells **strongly chemical — ammonia, kerosene-like, or distinctly different from previous cans of the same SKU**.

Genuine Tractor Emulsion has a slight off-white tone that looks consistent across cans. Counterfeits often show colour drift — slightly more grey, slightly more yellow.

**5. The coverage test**

Real Asian Paints Tractor Emulsion covers ~140 sqft/L per coat. Real Birla Opus Premium covers ~120 sqft/L. If a 4 L can only manages to cover one room of a 2 BHK at one coat, the paint has been diluted or filled with cheap base.

**6. Tinting machine check (for tinted shades)**

When you buy a tinted shade, the dealer should:
- **Tint in front of you** using a brand-authorised tinting machine
- Print a **shade card with batch code** that matches your can
- Provide a **printed bill** with the exact shade name and code

Buying pre-tinted "by the shop" off the shelf risks shade-fraud (cheap white paint tinted to look like a premium shade).

## Where counterfeiting is worst in Chennai

Based on our supply-chain experience:

- **Highest counterfeiting risk**: Cement and paint sold by the loose kilo, or in unbranded packaging, or through hand-cart vendors near construction sites
- **Medium risk**: Hardware shops in non-organised retail (small kirana-style hardware stores) — varies by shop reputation
- **Low risk**: Brand-exclusive outlets (UltraTech BUILD stores, Ramco Saaral, Asian Paints Colour stores), large organised retail (Berger, Birla Opus showrooms), and verified online channels (Suppliable, brand websites)
- **Lowest risk**: Direct from-factory pickup with original invoice (mostly for big bulk orders)

## What to do when you find a counterfeit

1. **Don't unload / don't open** the bag/bundle/can
2. **Photograph** packaging, batch code, and your bill
3. **Refuse the delivery** and note "Suspected counterfeit, batch verification failed" on the challan
4. **Demand replacement** with verified-batch stock
5. **Report** to:
   - Brand quality team (every major brand has a counterfeit reporting line — see contact list below)
   - **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)** at https://www.bis.gov.in for IS-marked product violations
   - **Local police** (counterfeiting is a criminal offence under the Trademarks Act 1999)
   - **Consumer forum** if you've already paid for and received fake material
